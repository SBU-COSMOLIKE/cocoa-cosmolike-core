# 23. One execution and memory plan for the mean and covariance

> **Archive status.** This source study records design proposals and validation requirements, not completed features. Read the [maintained implementation status](../threepcf.md) first. Both the mean and its covariance belong in `cosmolike/3pcf/`; earlier destination proposals using `fastnc/` or `covariances/` are superseded.

The proposed first implementation uses one process with one installed CosmoLike cosmology and up to ten OpenMP workers. Preparation, mean evaluation, covariance construction, and final matrix algebra are separate phases. Each phase has one owner of parallel work and a bounded workspace. This arrangement lets the mean and covariance share their physical inputs without retaining every intermediate array or allowing a parameter setter to replace data that workers are reading.

This chapter combines the reductions in [Chapter 19](19_further_covariance_reductions.md), [Chapter 20](20_further_data_vector_reductions.md), and [Chapter 21](21_further_execution_and_cache_design.md). It adds an allocation plan and source checks on arrays that can dominate memory. The pinned versions remain fastnc TATT `7b2336ce37a909bed51f07f32a2a4f096e71b6ce`, fastnc main `e59eab5a07401d2f484bfe00dd48827f2d2162c7`, Y6 `fd59fe4fee694d359453d5f573d6a4a55a47a812`, and CosmoLike core `0c0cf3838852396933c7f49550e35917dad0537c`, including its inspected local worktree. The source was read; no implementation, imports, numerical tests, or timing exercises were run.

## 1. Fix the observable before selecting its calculation

The measurement record must identify DES Y3 or Y6, the source distributions, source ordering, angular bins, aperture radii, triangle exclusions, mask, calibration convention, and covariance terms. Y3's 80 aperture entries and the inspected Y6 configuration's 100 entries remain distinct. The joint vector also contains the requested uncompressed two-point statistic. An aperture-variance reference cannot silently replace its measured $`\xi_\pm`$ bins or their cross-covariance kernels.

There are two proposed three-point calculation routes. The finite-estimator route retains the measured triangle grid and uses Chapter 21's dual weights to contract the fixed linear transform without reconstructing all natural components. The full-filter route uses the analytic angular or radial-product identities of Chapter 20. Its geometry is simpler, but agreement with the finite measured estimator is a scientific condition to establish. These routes are alternatives for the same contribution; their predictions must not be added. Ordinary forward natural-component evaluation remains available for comparisons, with its large arrays allocated only when requested.

Several improvements compose with either route: preparing power readers once, collecting the three TATT insertions, sharing radial weights, assigning independent outputs to workers, and vectorizing their arithmetic. Within the full-filter route, analytic opening-angle integration and Hankel-product integration are alternative evaluations of overlapping terms. BiHalofit's separable three-halo part and its residual one-halo fit can be evaluated by different methods and summed, but the nonlinear interpolation of the complete projected bispectrum must still be respected when reproducing the existing finite algorithm.

The covariance has analogous choices. The $`BB`$ common-side tables and the real-space connected three-field correlations describe the same partitions. Finite-window halo products and their large-area limit are two window prescriptions for the same physical term. The selected upstream long-mode term must be checked for overlap with a fuller super-sample response. A task list should name a physical contribution once, then name the chosen representation beneath it. This prevents a list of useful reductions from becoming a covariance with duplicated terms.

## 2. The sequence of preparation and evaluation

**Prepare the measurement once.** Build index maps, integration domains, angular interpolation stencils, compact coupling factors, filter weights, and the chosen transform machinery. Derive dual weights stage by stage when that route is selected; do not construct a giant matrix by transforming every possible input basis vector. A new measurement grid or filter changes these objects even if their dimensions happen to remain equal. A Y3 and a Y6 measurement may share a numerical table only when all its defining nodes and conventions agree.

**Install one physical model, then prepare its readers.** Set distances, growth, power spectra, baryonic prescriptions, and halo inputs with explicit units and density-field conventions. Finish all first-use table construction before launching workers. Prepare source weights and IA coefficients after the cosmology-dependent distance conversion. The core's halo driver follows this order: it warms variance, bias, concentration, and NFW readers before its OpenMP regions. [Serial reader preparation](../../../../../cosmolike/covariances/halo_cov.c).

**Build the fiducial covariance in stages.** Compute the needed pair correlations, filtered bispectrum functions, halo-product transforms, and selected responses in bounded blocks. Reduce each block into final matrices or reusable small radial functions. Compute a compatible fiducial mean while its shared physical inputs are available, then save the final vector. Preserve independent radial integrals in products of projected spectra; sharing a node grid does not turn two independent redshifts into one. Assemble all requested blocks with the same measurement ordering, check the matrix, and retain the declared fixed covariance and its chosen solve representation outside the mutable model workspace.

**Evaluate the mean and its derivatives.** Keep the fiducial physical basis while processing compatible nuisance changes. Refill source or IA coefficients as required; multiplicative-only shear calibration can act on the final uncalibrated vector. For a cosmological displacement, replace the affected physical tables and queries, compute its mean, and save the small final vector. The fixed fiducial covariance remains unchanged. A covariance derivative is a different requested operation and needs a new covariance evaluation. Schedule Y3 and Y6 source projections together only where they can share the same physical model and nodes.

This order does not require every covariance intermediate to survive the Fisher calculation. If a later covariance robustness run uses another halo or window prescription, its cache key selects the relevant tables to rebuild. It does not require retaining the previous model's entire halo workspace merely to save its final matrix.

## 3. Allocation, refill, and lifetime are separate decisions

The table specifies the proposed ownership contract. “Capacity” means allocated storage; “valid” means the stored values match every defining input. Sufficient capacity alone never establishes validity.

| Object | Allocate or enlarge when | Refill when | Lifetime and release rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measurement maps, domains, angular stencils | Node counts, stencil sizes, or output count exceed capacity | Actual nodes, bounds, units, cuts, ordering, or interpolation convention change | Measurement lifetime; workers read immutable values |
| Compact coupling, Bessel factors, FFT plans | Orders, FFT dimensions, strides, alignment, or worker workspace require it | Grid origin/spacing, bias, averaging, boundary treatment, or plan settings change | Retain the selected backend's objects; build and destroy plans outside worker regions |
| Aperture dual weights or full-filter geometry | Number of retained filters/modes or transform nodes changes | Any operation included in the measurement map changes | Retain only the selected route; optional diagnostic route can be rebuilt |
| Model readers and physical arrays | Power, radial, mass, or physical-query capacities grow | Cosmology, supplied arrays, units, matter/IA power model, baryons, or halo prescription change | One active model; refill only after all readers finish |
| Source and IA coefficients | Bin/node/field-role capacities grow | Source values, photo-z parameters, distances, IA amplitudes or evolution change | One active nuisance state, or an explicitly bounded batch |
| Triangle, mode, and halo scratch | Tile dimensions or allocated worker count increase | Every task overwrites its used cells, including padding | Reusable allocation; no physics validity inferred from previous contents |
| Filtered covariance functions | Chosen retained function count and radial nodes change | Their physical inputs, source weights, integration bounds, or windows change | Retain only functions needed by later terms; release after final assembly unless a declared repeat uses them |
| Fixed covariance and solve object | Kept joint data length or storage layout changes | Covariance contents, fiducial definition, ordering, cuts, or correction changes | Forecast lifetime, independent of displaced mean state |
| Saved predictions and derivatives | Number of retained requests or data length grows | Exact requested prediction differs | Small bounded store keyed by exact parameters and numerical definition |

The existing global state imposes a firm boundary. `initial_setup` resets model structs, baryon state, supplied-table origin flags, and dispatch settings; it does not instantiate a second independent cosmology. New module caches need an initialization generation in their validity record so that matching parameter values cannot accidentally revive arrays from a previous setup with another provider. Owned measurement geometry can survive only through an explicit external owner and a verified unchanged key. Never keep a borrowed pointer into a reset model as a saved fiducial object. [Reset sequence](../../../../../cosmolike/generic_interface.cpp).

For normal derivative updates, use the defined setters rather than treating `initial_setup` as an inexpensive per-point operation. A full reset must reinstall all required inputs. Covariance preparation can also change the origin of FAST-PT tables, as discussed in Chapter 14; the following mean call must use the intended table provider. Retaining a covariance matrix externally avoids dependence on those internal arrays, but does not remove the need to prepare the mean's readers correctly.

## 4. One thread owner in each phase

| Phase | OpenMP work owner | Useful SIMD lane meaning | Shared input and private output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical triangle preparation | Triangle or radial blocks | Independent triangles at one radial node | Shared power reader; worker-owned power/kernel tile |
| Finite-estimator mode contraction | A mode block or output block, chosen once | Adjacent Fourier cells or independent filter outputs | Shared compact couplings and weights; disjoint block result |
| Diagnostic forward transforms | Independent mode tasks | Frequency multipliers outside the FFT | Shared plans and factors; private real/complex buffers |
| Filtered halo preparation | Halo-node/profile blocks | Wavenumbers, masses, or radii according to contiguous layout | Shared abundance/concentration; one writer per profile row |
| Covariance function preparation | Common-side or radial-function rows | Outputs sharing a spectrum or profile value | Shared physics tile; one ordered quadrature sum per output |
| Covariance assembly | Disjoint upper-triangle matrix tiles | Neighboring output entries | Shared radial functions; no competing writes to a matrix cell |
| Dense solve or factorization | One selected algebra scheduler | Library-managed arithmetic | OpenBLAS remains single-threaded, as required by the core |

Ten outer workers initially imply single-thread FFT execution within each task and single-thread OpenBLAS. The core FFTLog workspace demonstrates the required separation: it keeps one scratch row per worker, shared forward coefficients, and a block of 16 Mellin kernels. It explicitly rejects a larger subsequent thread team than it allocated. Copy this ownership rule, not its two-point physical kernel. [Workspace allocation](../../../../../cosmolike/covariances/fftlog_cov.c); [worker-capacity check](../../../../../cosmolike/covariances/fftlog_cov.c).

An inner helper that already creates an OpenMP region should run as its own phase, not be called concurrently from an outer ten-task loop. A fixed output can keep its quadrature additions in node order while SIMD handles different outputs. Where a mode sum is distributed, store partials in a fixed mode order and combine them in that order. Strict arithmetic and these ownership rules allow parallel work without assuming reassociation or global `fast-math` options.

## 5. Count the arrays that can actually coexist

Let $`N=N_1N_2`$ be the actual unpadded Fourier-grid area, $`m=M_{\max}+1`$ the number of nonnegative modes, $`l=L_{\max}+1`$ the number of bispectrum orders, $`N_\theta`$ the number of side bins, $`N_\phi`$ the opening-angle count, $`N_f`$ the number of aperture outputs per source triplet, and $`N_s`$ the number of triplets. Use eight bytes for a real double and sixteen for a complex double. The following are payload formulas, excluding alignment, pointer tables, allocator metadata, and provider memory.

| Possible array | Payload bytes | Decision in the proposed plan |
|---|---:|---|
| Expanded coupling over nonnegative modes | $`8lmN`$ | Avoid; retain unique-ratio values or generate a mode block |
| Compact coupling | $`8l n_M N_\psi`$ plus index map | $`n_M`$ is the stored mode count, possibly $`2m-1`$ in the upstream table |
| All interpolated bispectrum orders | $`8lN`$ per active triplet | Retain one triplet or a bounded output batch |
| All $`H_M`$ grids | $`8mN`$ per triplet | Replace by $`8b_MN`$ for a mode block of size $`b_M`$ |
| Aperture dual weights | $`8N_fmN`$ | Persistent for the selected real-input finite-estimator route; tile if necessary |
| Four natural-component mode arrays | $`32N_\theta^2(2m-1)`$ | Diagnostic forward route only |
| Four complex natural components | $`64N_\theta^2N_\phi`$ per triplet | Contract and release one triplet; multiply by $`N_s`$ only if retaining all |
| Original complex aperture weights | $`64N_fN_\theta^2N_\phi`$ | Needed to prepare or check dual weights; release when no longer used |
| Final joint covariance | $`8D^2`$ for joint length $`D`$ | Add separate inverse/factor and algebra work arrays actually retained |
| Saved mean/derivative vectors | $`8DN_{\rm saved}`$ | Bounded request store; usually preferable to retaining old physical grids |

These dimensions follow the source's allocations, not just an asymptotic argument. `compute` holds all $`H_M`$ and four mode arrays before creating the angular arrays. Rafael's backend then creates a convention-converted copy and retains it for each requested key. Its aperture integrator separately stores complex weights. A streaming C interface can avoid that simultaneous retention by accepting one triplet, immediately contracting it, and returning its small aperture vector. It must still return independent arrays when a caller explicitly requests natural components. [Mode and angular allocations](https://github.com/git-sunao/fastnc/blob/7b2336ce37a909bed51f07f32a2a4f096e71b6ce/fastnc/fastnc.py#L335-L405); [backend copies](https://github.com/rchgomes/desy6-3pcf/blob/fd59fe4fee694d359453d5f573d6a4a55a47a812/desy6_3pcf/fastnc_backend.py#L268-L281); [aperture weights](https://github.com/rchgomes/desy6-3pcf/blob/fd59fe4fee694d359453d5f573d6a4a55a47a812/desy6_3pcf/map3.py#L98-L126).

For an illustrative actual grid of $`150\times150`$, $`l=51`$, $`m=101`$, $`N_\theta=25`$, $`N_\phi=100`$, and $`N_f=5`$, the dual weights occupy 90.9 MB, all $`H_M`$ 18.18 MB, interpolated orders 9.18 MB, mode arrays 4.02 MB, and one angular output 4.00 MB. Twenty saved angular outputs occupy 80 MB; their aperture weights occupy another 20 MB. An expanded nonnegative-mode coupling alone would occupy 927.18 MB. These are illustrative decimal-MB allocations, not a measured peak or recommended resolution. The configured minimum FFT size does not determine the actual tuned size.

The source already deduplicates angular ratios in its optional coupling table, and stores both signs of $`M`$. On an ideal identical logarithmic grid, ratios are functions of the index difference, giving $`2N_1-1`$ distinct ratios. An index-based representation could exploit this, but must reproduce the actual tuned grids and floating-point convention; do not infer that `np.unique` necessarily produces that exact count. [Actual compact table](https://github.com/git-sunao/fastnc/blob/7b2336ce37a909bed51f07f32a2a4f096e71b6ce/fastnc/coupling.py#L195-L239).

Worker FFT scratch adds $`T[8rN_{\rm pad}+16cN_{\rm half}+16eN_{\rm extended}]`$ bytes, where $`T`$ is the allocated worker count and $`r,c,e`$ count the simultaneously live real, half-spectrum, and extended-complex arrays chosen by the implementation. The upstream two-Bessel routine expands its real FFT coefficients to a larger conjugate representation, so counting only the initial half-spectrum understates its memory. A C design may avoid that expansion, but must derive equivalent endpoint handling first. Plans and library workspaces are additional. [Coefficient extension](https://github.com/git-sunao/fastnc/blob/7b2336ce37a909bed51f07f32a2a4f096e71b6ce/fastnc/twobessel.py#L73-L106).

## 6. Mellin compression and halo tiles need separate budgets

Main's Slepian backend is an optional alternative, not a required allocation in the initial TATT port. Its regular Mellin tensor has shape $`(N_d,N_e,N_\theta,N_\theta)`$, costing $`16N_dN_eN_\theta^2`$ bytes per retained kernel. Ideal rank-$`r`$ storage costs $`N_\theta^2[16r(N_d+N_e)+8r]`$. With illustrative $`N_d=N_e=256`$, $`N_\theta=25`$, and $`r=16`$, those are 655.36 MB and 82.00 MB respectively. Neither count includes the other kernels that the cache may retain. [Declared shapes](https://github.com/git-sunao/fastnc/blob/e59eab5a07401d2f484bfe00dd48827f2d2162c7/fastnc/threepcf/slepian.py#L133-L207).

The current construction does not obtain the smaller peak automatically. It first builds the dense tensor, performs a full batched SVD, slices the factors to the retained rank, and reconstructs a dense tensor to assess the error. The slices are views, and the receiving dataclass uses `np.asarray` without requesting independent compact copies; they can retain full factor allocations. During reconstruction-error evaluation, the dense input, both full complex factors, reconstructed tensor, and subtraction temporary can coexist. The illustrative square case therefore has roughly 3.28 GB in those arrays and singular values alone, before LAPACK workspace. A port should construct bounded angular-pair blocks and own compact retained factors explicitly. Compression error must still be assessed in the predicted vector and its derivatives. [Compression and reconstruction](https://github.com/git-sunao/fastnc/blob/e59eab5a07401d2f484bfe00dd48827f2d2162c7/fastnc/threepcf/slepian.py#L1181-L1212); [dense build before compression](https://github.com/git-sunao/fastnc/blob/e59eab5a07401d2f484bfe00dd48827f2d2162c7/fastnc/threepcf/slepian.py#L1700-L1728).

For covariance, retain only an active halo block. With $`b_z`$ radial shells, $`N_k`$ wavenumbers, and $`N_h`$ mass nodes, the core's profile block costs approximately $`8b_zN_k(N_h+1)`$ bytes. Its source prepares abundance and concentration once for that block and distributes profile rows among workers. Filtered profiles for a proposed block of $`b_h`$ halo nodes, $`N_R`$ radii, and $`N_u`$ offset samples cost $`8b_hN_RN_u`$. A block of $`N_p`$ pair/triple products costs another $`8b_hN_pN_u`$; transformed products add their own wavenumber samples and transform scratch while both representations coexist. Generate, transform, and reduce product groups before moving on, instead of storing every source-labeled product at every mass and redshift. Source-independent pure-lensing profiles can receive their source weights during projection. [Core profile allocation and work division](../../../../../cosmolike/covariances/halo_cov.c).

Retained covariance functions also need accounting: $`8N_KN_r`$ for real pair-correlation rows, $`8N_SN_q`$ for real filtered-bispectrum spectra, and their window/projection arrays. Here $`N_K`$ and $`N_S`$ count distinct required functions under the actual field and filter labels, not all possible combinations. Independent redshift integrals and IA field roles can enlarge these counts. Use the source-factor reductions only where their assumptions hold. The joint covariance dimension is $`D=N_\xi+N_{\rm aperture}`$; counting only its 100-entry aperture block would omit the larger matrix and cross-block work.

## 7. A bounded peak and eviction policy

For sequential phases, the planned peak is the persistent allocation plus the largest simultaneous phase allocation:

```math
M_{\rm peak}^{\rm planned}=M_{\rm persistent}
+\max_{s\in\{\mathrm{preparation,mean,covariance,algebra}\}}
\left(M_s+M_{{\rm boundary},s}\right).
```

The persistent part includes selected measurement geometry, retained model/provider tables, the fixed covariance object, and saved predictions. Each phase term includes all its simultaneously live shared arrays and worker scratch. Preparation includes temporary arrays used to build dual weights or compressed kernels. Boundary memory for that phase includes packed return copies, Python-owned arrays still referenced by callers, and any old/new buffers that coexist during publication. Each allocation belongs to one category, so a retained model table is not counted again as phase scratch. Library workspaces and allocator overhead must be added when known. The equation describes the proposed lifetime discipline; it is not the measured peak of either upstream repository.

Set a byte cap for optional caches before allocating them. Pin active inputs and required scratch. First evict diagnostic angular arrays and completed product tiles, then obsolete model-dependent tables, then rebuildable optional geometry for inactive backends. Keep small final predictions and the declared fiducial covariance. If required storage still exceeds the cap, reduce tile or retained-mode block size, or use fewer concurrent workers with larger per-task storage. Do not silently lower quadrature accuracy, change a halo model, truncate physical covariance terms, or introduce an unvalidated rank to fit memory. An in-place refill can avoid old/new double buffering only after all readers finish and the object's validity is cleared until completion.

Packed layout matters at the C/Python boundary. Core `malloc2d` pads each double row to 64 bytes and includes a row-pointer table. Its storage is not a tightly packed NumPy matrix with the logical column count. Account for a packed output copy or expose correct strides with a lifetime owner; a view into reusable worker scratch is not a valid persistent result. A copy is not inevitable: the current production covariance binding borrows contiguous inputs and allocates Python-owned outputs that C fills directly. That pattern is suitable for the small returned aperture vector and final covariance. [Padded allocation](../../../../../cosmolike/basics.c); [production array ownership](../../../../../cosmolike/covariances/components_interface_cov.cpp).

Several independent model processes multiply private provider tables, caches, and workspaces. Ten single-worker processes are therefore a different memory plan from one ten-worker process. The first design uses the latter. A later process/thread comparison must keep the total core allocation controlled and include every replica; operating-system page sharing must not be assumed to survive model updates.

The user's approximately 30-second existing calculation and willingness to spend up to roughly 50 minutes on covariance define useful operational context. They do not establish the port's runtime. A fixed covariance is paid once per chosen fiducial model, whereas the mean is repeated across derivative requests. The plan therefore prioritizes reliable covariance term coverage, shared physical preparation, and low repeated mean cost. A future quiet-machine measurement must include setup, covariance terms, matrix algebra, copies, and peak memory; no CUDA dependency or timing assumption is needed to define this CPU execution plan.
